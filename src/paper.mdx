<section id="abstract" class="paper-section">

<h2 id="abstract-heading">Abstract</h2>

Short monocular videos reveal only a limited range of a deformable object's material responses. Different constitutive models can therefore match the observed motion yet predict divergent behavior under new interactions. We propose **PrismPhys**, a vision-language-guided physics-search framework that treats constitutive families as competing hypotheses and jointly searches their continuous parameters.

To explore heterogeneous material mechanisms, a frozen vision-language model proposes simulator-valid hypotheses from observed and simulated motion, while measured source-action simulation responses guide their evaluation and iterative refinement. Candidates are compared using image, motion, and projected-shape residuals. By selecting the material model from source observations, our framework enables 3D motion prediction under unseen interactions without target-specific calibration.

This ambiguity exposes an evaluation gap: conventional metrics cannot directly assess whether methods distinguish materials with similar observed motion but different responses under new interactions. To evaluate this capability, we introduce **AmbiPhys-Bench**, a controlled cross-interaction benchmark, together with **cross-interaction future discrimination accuracy (CFDA)**. CFDA tests whether each predicted future is closer to its own material's response than its counterpart's. Experiments show improved cross-interaction prediction and material discrimination over competing methods, while real-world videos support unseen-future 3D motion prediction.
